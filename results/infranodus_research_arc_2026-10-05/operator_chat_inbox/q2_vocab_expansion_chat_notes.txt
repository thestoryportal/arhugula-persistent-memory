Elaboration: new-token recall as a pre-training diagnostic

The statement's core claim. Current vocabulary-extension work measures initialization quality indirectly — perplexity after N training steps, downstream task accuracy, translation BLEU. These are all confounded by subsequent training. A recall probe asks something stricter and cheaper: at step zero, before any gradient update, can the model produce the new token at all?

Concretely, the experiment. You add token `ĥ` for a string that previously tokenized as `[h₁, h₂, h₃]`. You initialize `E[ĥ]` by some projection `f(E[h₁], E[h₂], E[h₃])` — mean, learned hypernetwork (HYPEROFA), attention-aware distillation (Token Distillation), alignment-based (TokAlign). Then, in contexts where the old pipeline would emit `h₁`, you measure the rank of `ĥ` in the output distribution.

Why this is diagnostic rather than redundant. Three failure modes separate out that perplexity collapses together:

Dead token — `ĥ` never ranks, embedding fell outside the active manifold. Norm mismatch, usually.
Cannibalized token — `ĥ` ranks top-1 everywhere, because the mean of constituents lands near a high-frequency attractor. This looks good in early perplexity and is a disaster later.
Shadowed token — `ĥ` ranks just below its own constituents `h₁...h₃`. This is the interesting case: the projection preserved semantics but not competitive mass. The new token inherits meaning without inheriting probability.

Mode 3 is invisible in every metric currently reported, and it's the one that actually determines how long adaptation takes.

The knowledge-editing bridge. Your graph puts `edit` as a gateway node with high betweenness but tiny degree — it's a hinge nobody is standing on. The connection: ROME/MEMIT-style edits insert a new fact whose object is often a rare or multi-token entity. The edit "succeeds" by the standard metric (the target string has high probability) while the entity token itself remains shadowed — which is exactly why edits fail to generalize to paraphrases. Edit success and token recall are the same measurement under different names. KLOD's locality-preservation and the token-overfitting work (2502.00602) are circling this without naming it.

What makes it publishable. It's a benchmark, not a method. Take the five existing initializers, run recall@k on held-out contexts at step 0, and correlate with final downstream performance. If recall@10 at initialization predicts convergence speed, you've given the field a 5-minute test that replaces a 5-GPU-day one. If it doesn't correlate, that's a more interesting paper — it means projection-based initialization works for reasons unrelated to the new token's own retrievability, and the whole "semantically meaningful initialization" framing is post-hoc.

Elaboration: the recall probe, and the gap it exposes

The sentence being elaborated was: new-token recall as a cheap pre-training diagnostic, which also bridges initialization and knowledge editing.

1. What "recall of the new token" actually isolates

Every existing initializer — mean-of-constituents, TokAlign/TokAlign++ alignment, HYPEROFA's hypernetwork, Token Distillation's attention-aware fit, KL-based self-distillation — produces a vector that looks right in embedding space. Cosine similarity to the constituent centroid is high. Nobody checks whether the model will ever emit it.

The probe: hold the model frozen, feed contexts where the old tokenization would produce `h₁`, and record the rank of the new token `ĥ`. Three distinguishable failures:

| failure | signature | why perplexity hides it |
|---|---|---|
| dead | `ĥ` never in top-1000 | the constituents still cover the string, loss barely moves |
| cannibal | `ĥ` top-1 everywhere | early loss improves, collapse arrives later |
| shadowed | `ĥ` ranks just under `h₁…h₃` | loss is fine; adaptation is just slow for unexplained reasons |

The third is the one that matters, and the one no current paper reports. Semantics transferred; probability mass did not. The projection carried direction but not norm-relative-to-competitors.

2. Why the editing link is not a metaphor

Your graph has `edit` with degree 5 but betweenness 0.033 — a hinge with almost nothing resting on it. That structural fact is the finding.

ROME, MEMIT, Persuasion Tokens, KLOD all insert a fact whose object is frequently a rare or multi-token entity. Edit success is scored as the target string gets high probability in the prompt template. But the token-overfitting paper (2502.00602) already shows this is heterogeneous across token positions — which is the shadowing failure under a different name. KLOD's non-target distribution preservation is, read structurally, a constraint on exactly the competitor-mass question the recall probe measures.

So: edit locality and initialization recall are one measurement. One field calls it "the edit doesn't generalize to paraphrases," the other calls it "the new token adapts slowly." Neither has noticed they're describing the same vector being insufficiently competitive against its own neighbors.

The Embedding-Virtualized Knowledge paper (2602.01977) is the nearest thing to this realization and still frames it as an evaluation problem rather than an initialization one.
