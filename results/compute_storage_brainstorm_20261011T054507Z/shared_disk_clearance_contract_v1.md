## C2. Selected mechanism and cooperative premise

Propose one M3-local authority record, one local serialization lock, and at most one active shared-disk transfer unit. Intel requests work; only a registered M3 executor can release a shared-disk unit to perform I/O. Each unit is a finite, independently checkpointable part of a reviewed job. Starting another unit requires another affirmative M3 decision. This trades transfer throughput for a small admission/lifecycle surface. There is no distributed database lock, Intel-side science watcher or automatic transfer restart.

The future transport brief must demonstrate that every shared-disk operation crosses this M3 entry boundary. Intel must have no independent bulk-access route, writable memit mount, surviving file-service handle or credential that can bypass it. The M3 executor may send/receive a bounded stream to an Intel-local endpoint; after verified local closure, that endpoint can work only on its local bytes. An existing transfer interface that cannot satisfy this premise is **not admissible under v1**; review another interface rather than silently assuming containment. Scoped authenticated paths and measured cable behavior are future obligations (`B/design.md:208,297–299`).

All participants must adopt this boundary, including manual source/backup/retrieval work. The existing compute gate is a separate local admission check, not proof of transfer absence or complete cooperative adoption. Neither niceness nor zero command matches proves disk quiescence. These distinctions address the coverage and ownership failures in `r3:26`, `r4:28`, and `r5:43–49`.

## C3. Allowance record, location, grammar and validation

Proposed M3-only root: `/Users/robertrhu/Library/Application Support/LLMDB/shared-disk-r2/`; authority file `authority.json`, local lock `authority.lock`, immutable receipts under `receipts/`. These are small control metadata on M3 internal storage, never bulk staging or an archive. Exact permissions, metadata ceiling, retention without unauthorized deletion, runtime locations and durability primitives remain a reviewed implementation freeze. Authority and executable availability must not depend on memit or Intel (`r3:20`; `r4:41`; `B/design.md:241`). No directories are created by this draft.

MAESTRO's M3 coordinator is the sole authority writer. Workers send authenticated requests/acknowledgments; only the coordinator validates and incorporates them. Intel may keep a read-only dated response/receipt copy; it is never a second authority. A response authorizes only the named M3 executor through C4's local consume step, not Intel-initiated direct disk access. This supplies the consumer boundary required by `RE/phase1_intel_amendment_v1.md:42–48`.

IDs/nonces/epochs use exactly 32 lowercase hexadecimal characters and are unique within the retained authority history; counters are nonnegative integers; sizes are nonnegative integer bytes; paths are absolute, normalized and match the frozen allowed paths without traversal or implicit expansion. The future schema must specify field applicability for each discriminated state; omitted required ownership/checkpoint fields cannot mean an empty population. Authentication and authorization of host identity are mandatory; a self-reported ID or copied hash is not authentication. Input grammar and failed-write controls must be concrete (`r4:45`; `r5:35–41,75–81`).

Readers require successful authenticated observation of the current M3 authority, full parse/schema validation, matching reviewed identities, permitted state and complete lifecycle evidence. The M3 consume step also validates the current nonce/sequence and `now < consume_by_elapsed` on the same boot. A cached Intel response, stale observation, unavailable clock, malformed inventory, failed observation or unknown coordinator means **NOT GRANTED**. A successfully parsed empty process response alone is not valid absence evidence (`r5:79–81`).

All authority transitions serialize under the same M3 lock. A committed transition requires complete write, atomic replacement and verified file/directory durability under the selected host primitives. Do not acknowledge success or release a child to I/O before all steps succeed. Creation, rename, durability, lock or receipt failure refuses that invocation; no fallback to an older apparent `OPEN`. A failed science-pending write blocks that science invocation even if old authority still looks open. A failed allowance write cannot start a transfer. If a replacement may have occurred but completion is uncertain, treat authority as `RECOVERY_REQUIRED`; retain already active work as unresolved until C8 reconciliation (`r3:24`; `r4:45`; `r5:77–81`).

Expiry revokes only unconsumed permission; it never proves an active transfer ended. An expired active unit remains an exclusion until positively closed or reconciled. Restart, epoch change, pause, cancellation or science intent invalidates unconsumed grants; an active unit remains accounted for across those events (`r5:26–41,57–61`).

## C4. Pending science, ordering and allowance consumption

The science path makes a local M3 request with a fresh launch ID before starting any descendant able to dispatch science. Under the lock the coordinator durably registers `PENDING`, moves authority to `SCIENCE_PENDING`, invalidates all unconsumed allowances and records a pause request for an active unit. Duplicate live launch IDs refuse. Failed registration means no dispatch. This is the science signal; dashboard or remote status is advisory (`B/astra_r1_verdict.md:24`; `IM:171,179,187`).

Each transfer request and each allowance consume operation takes that same lock and rereads authority. Grant/consume refuses any nonterminal science registration, recovery state, unresolved transfer, invalid observation, wrong identity or failed disk budget. The executor and its dispatch-capable helpers register **before** they can touch shared disk; the coordinator commits `ACTIVE` before releasing the first I/O capability. A delayed go-message or not-yet-started child remains owned and blocks absence until cancelled and proved unable to start. No anonymous helper or detached/unregistered I/O child is permitted (`r4:30`; `r5:37–49`).

Race rule: the first successfully durable local transition orders the requests. If science pending commits before transfer consumption, science wins and the transfer cannot start, even with an earlier `GRANTED` response. If transfer `ACTIVE` commits first, that one unit may run; science then wins over all subsequent units and obtains clearance only after C5 proof. Simultaneous requests do not create a third path. Do not wait for Intel, perform network I/O or run an inventory while holding this ordering lock; any external observation used for clearance is revalidated at the final local transition. There is no pre-publication-only absence exception, including on timeout (`r3:18`; `r4:26`; `r5:10`).

For ordinary science with no outstanding shared-disk work, local valid authority and local proof suffice: **no Intel query, acknowledgment or reachability test is a prerequisite**. Intel-local compute continues. Only an actually unresolved shared-disk lifecycle can withhold R2 clearance; a disconnected idle Intel machine cannot (`B/design.md:216,233–235`; `RE/phase1_intel_amendment_v1.md:46–48`).

## C5. Pause request, verified acknowledgment and science clearance

Pending publication prevents new units immediately. It sends the matching job/unit/epoch/pause ID to the M3 executor and, when reachable, the Intel worker. The active executor must stop at the profile's verified safe boundary and must not start another unit. For a read, the boundary closes all source descriptors and I/O helpers and records the exact completed range/source identity. For a write, it also finishes or isolates the incomplete write, completes required flush/close operations, and records an immutable manifest/checksum checkpoint without claiming a partial object complete. No interrupted scientific evidence is overwritten or deleted. The transfer runtime profile must demonstrate its actual boundary; a progress count, sleeping process or pause-request delivery is insufficient (`B/design.md:235`; `B/astra_r1_verdict.md:24`; `r5:49,81`).

The acknowledgment names the allowance, unit, authority epoch, pause ID, boundary/checkpoint hash, source generation, completed versus incomplete bytes, and every remaining local/remote helper. It states that all shared-disk I/O has ended and all capabilities able to start more have been revoked/closed. M3-owned shared-disk endpoints and descendants must be independently accounted for. Intel acknowledges its worker state when reachable, but Intel's receipt alone never proves cessation on M3. Conversely, independently proved M3 endpoint/capability closure may acknowledge the **shared-disk phase** even if delivery to Intel fails; show Intel state `UNKNOWN` and leave its local completion unverified. This route requires the no-bypass premise in C2 to have passed real-pair acceptance (`B/design.md:233–235,299`; `RE/astra_intel_a1_verdict.md:9`).

After pending is durable, the coordinator validates the acknowledgment and obtains a fresh successful M3 observation of the complete owned I/O/capability population. If no unit was consumed, invalidate any `GRANTED` entry as `CANCELLED` under the lock and positively verify that no executor/capability was released; no remote acknowledgment is needed for that branch. It then revalidates unchanged epoch, transfer lifecycle and pending launch identity under the lock. Only then can it commit `PAUSED`/`COMPLETED` (or the proved never-started cancellation) and issue a single-use `CLEARED` nonce for that launch. Science clearance is never a pause request, an expired allowance, a zero-match classifier result or an old absence receipt. Any launch-capable/I/O-capable descendant still alive, unknown ownership, outstanding write/flush or unresolved endpoint withholds clearance (`B/astra_r1_verdict.md:24`; `r4:28`; `r5:43–61,79–81`).

If acknowledgment never comes, is interrupted, or cannot be verified by C6's deadline, the result is `HALTED_UNCLEARED`. Keep pending and unresolved transfer evidence; dispatch no science. A signal, an attempted stop or a deadline firing never substitutes for confirmed cessation. An operator may investigate and reconcile under C8; merely recording the failure cannot clear science (`r4:41`; `r5:26–33,51–61`).

## C6. Deadlines and bounded failure outcome

Exact latency is deliberately **OPEN**, not a made-up numeric promise: the approved design requires later freeze and real-interface verification (`B/design.md:235`). Before implementation, the permitting profile must freeze numeric `D_decision` (entry-to-terminal decision), `D_pause` (science request entry-to-verified pause), `D_unit` (maximum admitted shared-disk unit), grant-consume lifetime and clearance-consume lifetime. One M3 monotonic origin at science-request entry covers lock acquisition, pending mutation, request delivery, observations, checkpoint verification, final durability and decision delivery; no branch resets the origin (`IM:173,182,189`; `r4:32–39`; `r5:51–55`).

No coordinator operation may block indefinitely on network, stdout, filesystem writes or child completion. The chosen local launch guard must deny dispatch by `D_decision` even if the coordinator dies or never replies. Late results are unusable. Successful `CLEARED` requires receipt and mutation completion before the deadline. If output/receipt storage blocks, refusal remains refusal; receipt availability is unverified and cannot grant work. All outstanding mutation/observation descendants remain contained and cannot later publish a usable grant or clearance; epoch/nonce invalidation and recovery fencing must cover them. Freeze the actual cancellation/containment mechanism and prove it before claiming command return or writer cessation is bounded (`r5:51–55,88`).

The promised terminal safety outcome is **clear by verified boundary or halt uncleared within the frozen decision bound**. v1 does not promise that a hung kernel I/O can always be killed in that bound. A surviving I/O process keeps the transfer unresolved and science denied. On coordinator death no further unit is admitted; the independently running unit executor follows its frozen unit/stop profile, and unresolved work remains excluded. A failure to demonstrate the profile's bound is a failed acceptance case, not justification for relaxing it. This replaces the old unsupported whole-RE-termination promise with shared-disk exclusion and bounded refusal (`B/astra_r1_verdict.md:24,45–47`; `r5:26–33,51–55`).

## C7. Science launch, completion, release and explicit resume

R2 clearance is a separate local prerequisite; it does not change, replace or relax `tools/c10w_p2_gate.py`. That gate classifies command executables/exemptions, rejects failed process observation, checks tmux with its existing error rules, and combines fingerprint/GPU/dispatch checks (`tools/c10w_p2_gate.py:22–54,73–84,151–158`). R2 ownership uses the registered runtime identities in C9, never edits those classifiers. The M3 executor and helpers must have finished before affected science admission; runtime forms must be checked against the unchanged gate (`B/design.md:216,269`). No gate is executed by this draft.

Immediately before actual dispatch the science path locally consumes the matching nonexpired clearance nonce under the lock and commits `RUNNING`; it then runs its existing admission/dispatch checks. Pending/running exclusion stays durable through the gate-to-dispatch interval. Failure of an existing check leaves a registered, nondispatching launch that must cancel explicitly; it is not implicit release. Every dispatch-capable science helper is registered before release to run. A cached clearance cannot be reused for another launch or after a boot/epoch change (`r5:35–41,57–61`).

Release requires either `COMPLETED` with confirmed launch quiescence, or `CANCELLED` with confirmed inability to dispatch. It requires the registered launcher and every descendant that could dispatch/run science to have ended or have an independently verified irrevocably closed capability. There must be no delayed start message or queued retry capable of launching later. Coordinator exit, a compute-classifier snapshot or an unstarted launcher is insufficient. If ownership or lifecycle is unknown, mark `UNRESOLVED`; keep exclusion. Multiple pending launches retain exclusion until each is terminal. Only after durable terminal evidence may the coordinator make authority `OPEN` (`IM:180,187`; `r4:28`; `r5:35–41`).

**No automatic resume.** Science release, restored authority, reconnected cable, waking Intel, a timer, retry loop or a new coordinator never restarts a paused transfer. The sole permitted resume rule is: MAESTRO explicitly reviews the paused job ID, checkpoint/source identity, remaining byte/peak budget, current physical-pool observations, closed old capabilities and current science set; records `RESUME_APPROVED` with those identities; then grants a fresh single-use allowance under C3–C4. The worker validates that allowance and starts a newly registered unit. Changed source identity requires a fresh job generation/output namespace and review; the old checkpoint cannot authorize it. Completed/cancelled jobs cannot be resumed. Resume approval without a fresh valid allowance cannot start work (`IM:192`; `B/design.md:265,299`; `RE/astra_intel_a1_verdict.md:18–20`).

## C9. Ownership, command identity and observation

The runtime profile must enumerate every permitted M3/Intel invocation and helper, including administrative, transfer, network, capture, verification, stop and error paths. Before spawning dispatch/I/O-capable children, register host/boot, job/unit/launch token, executable and exact argument structure, PID plus start identity, parent/group identity and capability scope. A parent or group leader dying cannot erase surviving members. Registration/ownership errors refuse startup; failed registration must leave no released I/O child (`IM:172,188`; `r4:24,30`; `r5:43–49`).

Use executable and structured argument identity with host/boot/start identity; do not classify by path/token substrings anywhere in a command. A Codex/Claude review brief containing complete coordinator paths is descriptive text, not a coordinator. PID reuse or identity mismatch means unknown ownership and no unsafe signal, never absence. Neither matching patterns nor their zero-match output is the commissioning oracle: an independent launched-process/endpoint record must account for the entire population, including orphaned and intentionally untagged helpers. Observation must have successful status, validated structure, current source host/boot/epoch/time and independent positive controls (`r5:43–49,63–67,79–81`).

Exact process/descriptor observations, authentication, capability containment and actual M3 command forms remain **OPEN runtime-profile items**. No claim that a process group alone prevents escape or that a command-line marker alone authorizes signalling is made. A profile unable to prove local shared-disk closure cannot use the disconnected-Intel clearance route (`B/design.md:216,269,297–299`; `RE/phase1_intel_amendment_v1.md:80`).

## C10. Physical disk budget

Keep memit steady-state free-space target **at least 100 GiB** and warning **below 80 GiB**. Independently require observed free bytes on each affected physical pool to cover the job's projected additional peak plus **20 GiB**, including partial outputs, verification staging, temporary duplication and existing commitments. Count a physical pool once; a target or per-area quota is not an independent reservation (`B/design.md:165–169`; `IM:96`).

At grant and resume, require current identified-pool observations and the peak budget, subtract already committed additional peaks without double-counting bytes already present. Include registered pending science's frozen storage requirement before admitting support writes. Warn below 80 GiB; do not treat 100 GiB as sufficient for a larger preregistered peak. Preserve the binding per-job threshold rather than inventing a new science-gate classifier or a blanket science veto from the steady-state target. Unknown capacity, free space or peak means no new affected work. A read-only transfer can have zero new memit writes but must budget Intel growth and any actual temporary M3 writes. Exhausted support budgets pause the support job; they do not authorize deletion, borrowing M3 internal bulk space or changing a frozen science prereg (`B/design.md:165–169,216,241`; `IM:96`).
