You are an **independent, adversarial reviewer** of a research step in the LLM-as-Database program. You are not the author. **Do not rubber-stamp.** Your job is to find where the work is wrong, over-claimed, confounded, or drifting from the goal — *before* it is leaned on.

Run this review and answer concisely and specifically:

1. **Real criterion.** Does this answer the question's *actual* criterion, or a flattering adjacent one? (e.g. is the metric matched to the claim — top-1 for read-correctness, distributional for distributional; is the gate's true requirement being met or a softer proxy?)

5. **Drift check.** Does this advance the F1 readiness determination (or a live §0.3 falsifier)? If not, say so.

**Output:** a short verdict — `PROCEED` / `FIX-FIRST` / `OVERTURNED-OR-RECONSIDER` — then the **single most important thing** to do next, then the specific issues in priority order. Be a skeptic; the author will weigh your input heavily but evidence remains binding over your opinion.

## 1. Question and hypotheses

- **H-19 (span):** writing "<head> <modifier>" as two minted ids (init N) lands between the one-token mint and the multi-token string on the binding count, and the per-position curve shows the loss at the second id, so halving the span recovers part of the gap. Stub source: `hypotheses.md:194–196`.
- **H-19-null (pair novelty — the prior):** the two-id value lands with the string, because the second id must be predicted from a (head, subject) context the model has never seen and the solve supervises it only through the single key displacement (§2, "what the solve fits"); the F-M7/F-D4 seed sensitivity says a two-seed difference under 12/72 is within what the init alone produces (CORPUS/46:22, :33).
- **H-19-tradeoff:** the ordering holds but a locality gate, the perplexity gate, the write-size qualifier or a minted-id false-fire bound fails.

**Reading the stub honestly (departure 1 — the frozen A7 values cannot be split-minted).** The stub's "48 spare rows" (`prereg_stubs.md:41`) assumes 48 distinct words. On the frozen A7 values that is false:
- `build_stimuli` assigns heads by `coined_head[(i*2+1) % 24]` and modifiers by `coined_mod[(i*3+2) % 24]` (`c10e_bandknob.py:246–249`), which yields 12 distinct heads (each shared by two subjects) and 8 distinct modifiers (each shared by three).
- 8 of the 12 heads (Drennik, Vythorn, Vorlex, Kessivar, Halquin, Marsduq, Glimsto, Fluvane) are subject names in `FICTION` (`:57–61`), and three more (Lurmax, Brindu, Yastov) are prefixes of subjects.
- `register` adds tokens with `single_word=False` (`c10l_mint_lib.py:26`), so minting " Drennik" would retokenise every harness prompt containing that subject; step 0's P2a (identical tokenisation of every harness prompt, `c10l_mint_step0.py:63`, halt at `:70`) would fail by construction, and the subject key itself would become a random row.

**Resolution adopted for v1 (for the advisor pass to confirm or overturn):** a redrawn value set **V2** of 24 values = 24 distinct new heads × the 24 distinct `coined_mod` words (`:234–238`, injective assignment). The heads are authored under published rules — coined; no equality, prefix or substring relation with any `FICTION` subject, any L1/L3 country (`:277–278`) or any word of any harness prompt (`all_harness_prompts`, `lib:178–185`); each head ≥ 2 BPE pieces in the base tokenizer so the string baseline stays multi-token — published and SHA-256-hashed (`lib:174–175`) into step 0 before any run, with the overlap table reported. **All three arms run on V2 in the same session**, so the comparison is internal; CORPUS/45's numbers are context, not the comparator. The alternative (keep the frozen values, mint the modifier only and leave the head as BPE pieces) is a different hypothesis and is not adopted. The `coined_head` pool cannot supply the heads (only Qorvex and Vindex are subject-free), so the head list is new authored text and enters the manifest like a training phrasing (`prereg_stubs.md:12`).

## 2. Design (engine unmodified; the driver extends `c10l_mint_arms.py` through the existing `ARM_HOOKS` seam and the `register`/`set_rows` primitives; one step 0 per (arm kind, seed); five behavioural runs)

**Registration and initialisation (LAW#4 — `c10l_mint_lib.py`, read 2026-10-06).**
- `register(tok, values)` adds one `AddedToken(" " + v, normalized=False, lstrip=False, rstrip=False, single_word=False)` per string (`:24–27`); fails closed if fewer tokens were added than strings (`:27–29`, so a duplicate word aborts — V2 must have 48 distinct words); asserts the ids are the contiguous spare rows from `SPARE_START = 151665` (`:10`, `:30–32`; 271 spare rows to `VOCAB_ROWS = 151936`, `:11`, no resize) and that `tok(" " + v) == [id]` (`:33–36`).

- `build_init(..., kind="N", seed)` draws `torch.randn` per row from a `torch.Generator().manual_seed(seed)` and norm-matches to the base rows' mean norm (`:72`, `:84–86`, on CPU `:66`); the N rows do not depend on the constituent pieces, only on row order, so a 48-row N(seed s) is a fresh draw, not the 24-row one extended.

**What one request with a two-id target does in the solve (LAW#4 — `memit_dry_run/memit/memit/compute_z.py`).**
- The harness builds one request per fact, `{"prompt": CANON, "subject": c, "target_new": {"str": " " + value}}` (`c10e_bandknob.py:96–97`, `:480`), and `my_edit` calls `compute_z` once per request (`:191`).
- `compute_z` tokenises the whole target string (`:41–43`): for mint-2 that is `[ĥ_head, ĥ_mod]`; for mint-1 `[ĥ]`; for the string the BPE pieces. It builds the rewriting prompts as `context.format(prompt) + decode(target_ids[:-1])` (`:46–50`), i.e. the head id is appended to every context as text, and places both ids at the last two positions of the targets (`:60–65`).
- The optimised `delta` is added at the subject's last token only (`:98–99`, `lookup_idxs` `:68–73`); the loss is the per-position NLL summed and divided by the target length (`:149`) and averaged over prompts (`:150`), plus the KL term on `"{} is a"` (`:50`, `:127–128`, `:151–153`) and weight decay (`:154–156`); it stops when the total falls below 0.05 (`:164`) or at `v_num_grad_steps`; `delta` is clamped to a ball set by `target_init.norm()` (`:175–178`) and `z = target_init + delta` is returned (`:180`).
- **Consequence:** the second id is supervised only through the displacement at the key two tokens back, with the head id present in the context; a mint-2 miss at position 1 is therefore a statement about what one keyed residual can carry across a one-token gap, which is exactly the H-19 question. The "avg prob" the engine prints is `exp(−nll_each)` (`:162`), the geometric mean of the two token probabilities — this is what P3 reads.
