## 0. Where this sits, and what it is

The **§C write path**, read against the **§8.9 bar** (`spec:388`), on the **mint harness** of CORPUS/45: engine unmodified; the value's vocabulary representation is changed before the solve. CORPUS/45 removed the span entirely (one id per value) and lifted held-out transfer from 11 to 34–46/72, leaving F-M4 open: how much of the residual gap is the *length* of the value and how much the *novelty of the pair*. Arm A puts one point between the two ends on a redrawn value set: each coined word gets its own minted id, so the value is a two-id sequence whose second id is predicted from a context that already contains the first. Arm B puts a second point on the frozen values themselves: the head stays BPE pieces and only the modifier is minted, which asks whether the string arm's loss sits in its second word (§1). **The e2e map:** write layer, representation of the value at the tokenizer/embedding boundary (outside the §8.1 FFN compile, `spec:307`; §4). **In §0.3:** step 3 of the agreed sequence, a diagnostic lead, not a falsifier; it runs after steps 1–2 unless the operator reorders.

**What this experiment can and cannot conclude.** A pass advances a candidate representation. A miss at any stage means *this candidate configuration was not advanced* (one init kind, three seeds per arm, one value set per block, N = 24); it does not close H-19 or F-M4. The direction labels of §3 are a **routing signal for the sequence whose false-positive rate is UNVERIFIABLE from the historical totals** (Codex item 2); they identify nothing causally. Their power is low by construction (advisor v2 item 9): with S ≈ 11 and mint-1 seeds near {34, 36, 46}, SPAN-DIRECTION needs every mint-2 seed inside a window about four counts wide ([21, 24]) against a 12-count seed range, so the pre-registered outcome of block V2 under most true states is MIXED; what the block buys with certainty is the per-position curve and the bigram control, and a direction label only when the effect is large. CORPUS/45's qualifiers apply: not route viability, not an init-quality statement, no change to the F1 C10 status.

## 1. Question, hypotheses, and the value-set amendment

- **H-19 (span):** writing "<head> <modifier>" as two minted ids (init N) lands between the one-token mint and the multi-token string on the binding count, with the loss at the second id. Stub: `hypotheses.md:194–196`.
- **H-19-null (pair novelty — the prior):** the two-id value lands with the string: the second id must be predicted from a (head, subject) context the model has never seen, and the solve supervises it only through the single key displacement (§2); CORPUS/46 F-D4 says a difference under 12/72 is within what the init alone produces.
- **H-19-tradeoff:** the ordering holds but a locality, perplexity, write-size, false-fire or bigram gate fails.

**The frozen A7 values cannot be split-minted (departure 1, confirmed by both reviews).** `build_stimuli` assigns heads by `coined_head[(i*2+1) % 24]` and modifiers by `coined_mod[(i*3+2) % 24]` (`c10e_bandknob.py:246–249`): 12 distinct heads each on two subjects, 8 distinct modifiers each on three; 8 heads (Drennik, Vythorn, Vorlex, Kessivar, Halquin, Marsduq, Glimsto, Fluvane) are `FICTION` subjects (`:57–61`), three (Lurmax, Brindu, Yastov) are subject prefixes; `register` uses `single_word=False` (`c10l_mint_lib.py:26`). Codex's tokenizer-only check (`results/c10u_mint2_review_tokenizer_check_20261006-133250.json`, no model load): under the existing registration **33/96** CANON+PTEST prompts change tokenisation; with `single_word=True` **0/96** change but **0/24** target pairs encode as the two minted ids (and the 8 exact collisions remain — advisor item 2). Step 0's P2a (`c10l_mint_step0.py:63`, halt `:70`) would fail by construction.

**Resolution — both arms, by the operator's ruling D-C10r-rulings-4 (2) (`D-C10u-A7V2`; advisor item 2, Codex item 1 asked for the ruling).** The ruling makes the redraw and the fallback two arms of one pre-registration, each in its own comparison block (§2):

- **Arm A — A7-V2, two minted ids.** V2 = 24 values from 24 new heads × the 24 `coined_mod` words (`:234–238`, injective: head *i* pairs with modifier *i* for subject `FICTION[i]`). The heads are authored by the program session under the rules of §1.1, screened with the base tokenizer, and SHA-256-hashed (`lib:174–175`) into step 0 with the relation table. **What V2 changes, stated plainly:** (i) the head identities; (ii) the shared structure of the frozen set (12 heads × 2, 8 modifiers × 3) — in V2 every head uniquely identifies its modifier, which permits a head→modifier shortcut without continued subject dependence (Codex I-INFER), so the pairing problem itself is different and the §3 bigram control is mandatory; (iii) comparability — the frozen stimuli are the instrument behind every CORPUS/39–50 A7 number, and matching envelopes cannot restore it; what *is* matched is the head length, and the complete target length approximately (§1.1). All three arm-A conditions run on V2 in one block; CORPUS/45 is context, not the comparator. V2 is a scoped representation experiment: its pairing structure is not the frozen set's, the bigram control cannot restore that comparability, and no claim about the frozen set's pairing mechanism is made from block V2 (Codex v2 item 2). The redraw carries its own Decision-ID line, `D-C10u-A7V2`, in runbook §5.
- **Arm B — modifier-only minting on the frozen values.** The 24 frozen A7 values stay as they are; only their 8 distinct modifiers (Vask, Brakk, Stavik, Yornel, Spraxil, Ulvenn, Cindel, Drelth — `coined_mod[(i*3+2) % 24]`, in first-use order) are registered, so each target becomes the head's base BPE pieces followed by one minted id (3–4 ids; `results/c10u_mint2/v2_wordlist.json` → `modifier_only`). P2a-clean by construction: A7 value strings are excluded from `all_harness_prompts` (`lib:180`), the modifiers are not subjects, and the pre-screen finds 396/396 distinct inventory prompts unchanged. It keeps the frozen stimuli (CORPUS/45 comparability through the same-block mint-1 and A7-multi references) and forfeits the uniform two-id value: it tests whether the frozen string's loss sits in its *second word* (**H-19-B**: minting the modifier alone recovers the one-token mint's count) or in the multi-piece head (**H-19-B-null**: it gains nothing over the string). The three-word-per-modifier sharing of the frozen set is kept, so the §3 bigram control reads differently here (a modifier shared by three subjects cannot be a subject-independent continuation of one head, but can be of the head *word*, which is also shared by two subjects).

A non-word-boundary split of the frozen strings is not adopted (abandons "one per word", needs its own P2 audit). The two blocks are read side by side and never pooled: they are different stimuli.
