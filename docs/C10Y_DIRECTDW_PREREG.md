## 1. Question and hypotheses

- **H-Y1 (end-to-end objective — the claim under test).** Optimising each band layer's update `ΔW^l = A^l B^l` (B^l the closed form's own solution operator, held fixed; A^l free) against the target-token NLL **evaluated in the model carrying all five updates**, with the KL term on the essence prompt, raises A7 held-out full-sequence recall over the same-process closed-form write (recipe A), at the same locality, and not merely by writing larger.
- **H-Y2 (anchor closure — the research pass's stated premise).** Optimising the same parameterisation to close the residual to the engine's own target at the anchor (block-8 output at the subject-last token) raises recall. **Prior: NULL** — the canonical gap is already ≈ 2.8 % (§0), and removing the projector, which leaves 0.8 %, made canon worse (7 → 4, `CORPUS/44:29`).
- **H-Y0 (null).** Neither moves A7 outside the identical-baseline envelope.
- **H-Y3 (trade-off).** B-e2e gains, but through a larger write that leaks (the CORPUS/50 and /53 pattern: ×2.24 total write with `described_as` −4.2, `CORPUS/50:15`; ×2.05 with ten failed per-prefix gates, `CORPUS/53:29, :33`). The norm-matched control (§2.5) separates this from H-Y1.
- **Expectation stated now.** The nearest analogue the program has run is the in-place re-aim (CORPUS/50: 24/72, TRADEOFF). The likeliest outcomes for B-e2e are therefore TRADEOFF or a LARGE-WRITE-qualified gain; LEAD (≥ 30/72) is not the expected outcome. B-z is expected to sit inside [5, 18]/72 and within 5 of recipe A. DOW-KE's reported gains are CounterFact first-token metrics on real entities (Llama-3-8B generalization 96.64 vs AlphaEdit 94.22, Table 1), not this bar (`hypotheses.md:49`).

### 2.2 The parameterisation — DOW-KE §5 on this harness, derived from `my_edit:209–211`

DOW-KE §5 observes that the AlphaEdit closed form factors as `Δ = R B`, with R the allocated residuals and B "a solution operator built solely from the edit keys and the preservation statistics", and optimises a free `A^l` in place of R with B fixed. On this harness, for layer l with keys `K_l` (11008 × 24), residuals `R_l` (2048 × 24), cache `C_l` (zero for a fresh arm, `:474`) and projector `P_l`, write `M_l = K_l K_lᵀ + C_l` and `𝒜_l = P_l M_l + L2·I`. By linearity of the solve, `solve(𝒜_l, P_l K_l R_lᵀ) = X_l R_lᵀ` with `X_l = solve(𝒜_l, P_l K_l)` (11008 × 24), so

  **ΔW^l_A = R_l B_l,  B_l := X_lᵀ (24 × 11008).**

From `𝒜_l X_l = P_l K_l`: `X_l = P_l (K_l − M_l X_l)`, so X_l lies in the range of P_l (an orthogonal projector, `U Uᵀ` from the SVD at `:173–175`) and **`B_l k = 0` for every key k with `P_l k = 0`**, the covariance directions with singular value ≥ 0.005. Hence `ΔW^l = A^l B_l` is zero on the protected span **for every A^l** — the property DOW-KE builds into its parameterisation ("for any k ∈ span(U_τ), B^l k = 0; hence Δ^l k = 0 for every A^l", §5), here with the harness's own projector. The constraint is exact; it carries no penalty weight. Each update is rank ≤ 24 and lives in the closed form's own row space; the program measured R0's ΔW null-space share at 1.000 (`CORPUS/44:25`), and V7 (§3) requires the same of every optimised write.

**Capture solve.** `R_l` and `B_l` depend on the sequential state: layer l's keys and `cur` are read after layers < l were edited (`:196–201` follow `:217`). The driver therefore recomputes `my_edit`'s loop line for line (`:194–217`: same `compute_ks`, same `get_module_input_output_at_words` call, same `resid`, same float32 CPU solve, same fp16 addition) and records `R_l`, `X_l`, `B_l` per layer and arm; LAW#5 check (ii) (§2.8) proves this capture equals `my_edit`'s write. **Initialisation:** `A^l(0) = R_l`, so the zero-step write is recipe A's write exactly (check (iii)).

### 2.3 Objectives

- **B-e2e (DOW-KE, eq. "DOW-KE objective" in §5).** `L = L_edit + kl_factor · L_KL`, both evaluated on the model with all five updates applied as forward hooks on `down_proj` (the hook adds `(x·B_lᵀ)·A^lᵀ`, computed in float32 from `x.float()` and cast to fp16, to the fp16 module output). `L_edit` is built exactly as `compute_z` builds its rewriting loss: the same rewriting prompts (`compute_z.py:46–50`), tokenisation (`:53–57`), target placement (`:60–65`) and per-prompt / per-request means (`:149–150`), averaged over the 24 requests; NLL read from the final hidden state through `model.norm` and the tied head **at the target positions only** (memory, §2.10). `L_KL` is `compute_z`'s call (`:151–153`) on the 24 `"{} is a"` prompts at the subject-last index, with `kl_distr_init` the pristine model's distribution, computed once. **Gradient routing (DOW-KE §5, Table 2 row 4):** in `L_edit` only, for positions t before the subject-last index `s_i` of each rewriting sequence (`compute_z.py:68–73`), the hook uses `A^l.detach()`, so those positions reach A^l only through later keys; forward values are unchanged. The KL term uses the standard path.
- **B-z (anchor closure; doubles as the stub's sham, `prereg_stubs.md:76`).** Same parameterisation, init and optimiser; objective = closure of the engine's own anchor, as DOW-KE §3.2 defines it, across the same rewriting prompts: with `δ_i = z_i − h⁰_{i,0}` (the solved delta; `z = target_init + delta`, `compute_z.py:95, :180`) and `h_{i,j}` the block-8 output at the subject-last token of rewriting prompt j in the edited model, `L_z = mean_i [ Σ_j ‖h_{i,j} − (h⁰_{i,j} + δ_i)‖² / (J ‖δ_i‖²) ]` (J = number of rewriting prompts; `h⁰` pristine). On the identity template this is the squared remaining ratio of step 0. The forward may stop after block 8 (implementation-review item: a truncated forward or a full one; a full one only costs time). Gradient routing as in B-e2e.

### 2.5 Recipes and arms (A1, A2 and A7 under every recipe; one process runs all of them)

- **A — closed-form baseline**, the harness as it stands (`my_edit` untouched), with the driver's pre-solved targets supplied through `Z_OVERRIDE` (§2.6). Its A7 count is the comparator; its envelope is the LAW#3 gate.
- **B-z — anchor-closure / sham** (§2.3). Reproducing A within the envelope is the expected outcome; a gain revives the closure account on this harness.
- **B-e2e — DOW-KE proper** (§2.3). The candidate.
- **B-e2e-NM — norm-matched control** (run iff B-e2e's summed write ratio `Σ_l ‖ΔW^l_B‖_F / Σ_l ‖ΔW^l_A‖_F > 1.0` for that arm): B-e2e's stored per-layer updates rescaled to recipe A's per-layer Frobenius norms, `ΔW^l_NM = ΔW^l_B · ‖ΔW^l_A‖_F / ‖ΔW^l_B‖_F`, applied without optimisation. It asks whether B-e2e's direction carries the gain at the closed form's size (larger writes leak: `CORPUS/50:25`, `CORPUS/53:29–33`). When the ratio ≤ 1.0 the control is not run and the size question is moot.

4. **Wrapper.** `run_recipe` calls `my_edit` by module-global name (`:489`); the driver rebinds `H.my_edit` to a wrapper with a mode ∈ {`None`, `anchor`, `e2e`, `apply_stored`}. Modes `anchor`/`e2e`: capture solve → restore the band (`restore_layers`, `:155–159`) → optimise A^l with hooks → remove hooks → merge. Mode `apply_stored`: restore, add the stored NM updates. Each recipe is one `H.run_recipe(name, HP_BASE)` call (the `C10_RECIPES=candidate` semantics, `:582`), so every recipe runs its own LAW#5 gate and its own evaluation code path unchanged.
5. **Order in a process:** step 0 (§2.8 checks (iv)–(vii), memory and time smoke) → A → LAW#3 envelope check → B-z → B-e2e → B-e2e-NM (where triggered) → readouts. Process 2 repeats the whole sequence from the same input manifest (§2.7) and is both the reproducibility run and gate A.

### 2.7 Write-only ruling and the reproducibility rule

**The ruling, quoted** (`EXPERIMENT_RUNBOOK.md:330`, D-C10r-rulings-3 (2)): "Write-only ruling, the operator's words: training per commit defeats the spec's objective; likelihood of deploying it is essentially zero; run it only as a measurement — H-13/H-15 are an oracle measurement, not a route; H-18 demoted." The criterion it rests on (`recommended_sequence.md:11`): "The write must stay a per-commit solve that can be regenerated from the committed patch. Anything that needs a training set or a training loop per commit is out as a route (it breaks real-time use, the audit trail and compaction)."

**Why this writer qualifies as a write, on this program's reading.**
1. *Inputs are the patch and fixed artefacts only.* The loss is evaluated on exactly the strings `compute_z` already builds from the request — the canonical prompt under the frozen context prefixes plus the value's own tokens (`compute_z.py:46–50`) — and the `"{} is a"` KL prompt (`:50`). No paraphrase, generated text, held-out template or dataset enters the write. The fixed artefacts are the model revision, the covariance caches (SHA-256 per layer, `c10e:342–355`), the hparams file, the context-template file, this document's optimiser table, the driver's source hash and the seed.
2. *The engine already iterates per patch.* `compute_z` is a 25-step Adam loop per fact on the same prompts (`compute_z.py:104–172`). DOW-KE moves the optimisation variable from `delta` (2048 numbers per fact) to A^l (2048 × 24 per layer) and evaluates the same losses in the edited model (DOW-KE §5). What H-13/H-15 do and this does not: fit parameters to a training set of ≈ 20 phrasings per fact (`hypotheses.md:140`, `:158`).
3. *The update stays a closed-form-shaped overlay* on `down_proj`, rank ≤ 24 per layer, zero on the protected span (§2.2) — the D20 orthogonal projection is kept exactly (spec `:316`), and the covariance term enters through `B_l` (`𝒜_l` contains `KKᵀ + C`).
4. *The three harms the criterion names are measured, not assumed:* wall-clock per write against the 60-minute Write Lock TTL (spec `:783`) and the 2 h PREPARED default (spec `:678`) (real-time use); the reproducibility rule below (audit trail); regeneration in a fresh process from the same inputs (compaction re-runs the writer, e2e `:54`).
The research pass classed step 7 as "a per-commit, dataset-free write, though the result is slightly less reproducible than a closed form" (`recommended_sequence.md:27`), and the operator accepted the order containing it (`EXPERIMENT_RUNBOOK.md:330` (3)). The operator's words "training per commit" could still be read to include any per-commit gradient loop; that reading is the operator's to make (§6, decision 1).

**Reproducibility rule (the overlay-hash question, `council_critique.md:13, :88, :244`).** A closed-form ΔW is a function of (patch, covariance, hyperparameters) up to MPS forward noise; an iterative solve on MPS is not bit-reproducible (CLAUDE.md gate 2), so the `COMMITTED` overlay hash (spec `:671`) cannot be re-derived bit for bit. The record therefore carries: an **input manifest hash** `H_in` = SHA-256 over (the 24 request JSONs, model revision, covariance SHA-256s, context-template file SHA-256, hparams SHA-256, the §2.4 table as JSON, driver and reader source SHA-256s, seed); an **output hash** `H_out` = SHA-256 of the five fp16 update tensors; and a **regeneration certificate**: process 2 regenerates from `H_in` and the write counts as the same write iff R1–R3 (§3) hold. This is the experiment's proposal for how a ledger could own such a write; it is not a spec change, and adoption would need §11.5 / §10.2 wording (§4).

### 2.8 Inertness (LAW#5) and step-0 checks — numeric, same process; any failure → HALT (§5)

The known-ceiling reference for this new solve path is the same-process closed-form write (exact zero-step identity) and the A1 control (A1 ≥ 70/72 in every CORPUS/44 arm, `CORPUS/44:21`); the CORPUS/43 K1 ceiling named by the stub (`hypotheses.md:46`) is not re-run separately (review question, §6).
- **(i) Standing gate.** `run_law5` in every recipe (`:463`, rule `:444`) → INERT; |Δexpr| recorded (CORPUS/50: 0.0001; CORPUS/53: 0.0).
- **(ii) Capture-solve identity.** On identical restored weights, templates, P and pinned targets: run the original `my_edit` twice (floor = largest per-layer `‖ΔW_a − ΔW_b‖_F / ‖ΔW_a‖_F`), then the driver's capture solve; per-layer relative distance to `my_edit`'s write ≤ max(10 × floor, 1e-4) (the C10V (ii) rule, `docs/C10V_LAYERPICK_PREREG.md:58`), all three arms.
- **(iii) Zero-step identity.** Each B mode with T_max = 0 (A^l = R_l, merged) gives weights within (ii)'s tolerance of recipe A's and identical `canon_full_n`, `para_full_n`, `para_first_n`.
- **(iv) Hook-versus-merge parity at init.** With A^l = R_l: the hooked model (fp16 W + float32 low-rank term cast to fp16) and the merged fp16 model give identical greedy `full_seq_match` outcomes on ≥ 95/96 prompts (24 canonical + 72 held-out, A7); max |Δlogit| at the last position recorded.
- **(v) Projector property.** Null-space share `‖ΔW^l P_l‖²_F / ‖ΔW^l‖²_F` of the float32 update ≥ 0.999 at init and at the end of every B write.
- **(vi) Objective construction.** On the pristine model, the driver's per-request `L_edit` equals `compute_z`'s iteration-0 NLL for the same request (its printed value at delta = 0, `compute_z.py:159–163`, rounded to 3 decimals) within 2e-3, 24/24 for A7; the driver's tokenised batch, target positions and lookup indices are a line-for-line construction of `compute_z.py:41–73`. Micro-batching: the accumulated gradient over 4 × 6 requests against the full-batch gradient at init, cosine ≥ 0.999 per layer.
- **(vii) Gradient finiteness.** At init, the scaled gradient is finite on every layer at scale 2¹⁰ or after at most three halvings.
