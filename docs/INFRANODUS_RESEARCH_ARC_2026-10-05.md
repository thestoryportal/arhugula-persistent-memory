## 1. What the arc was asked and what it found so far

The operator ruled on 2026-10-05 that a side store is not F1 closure for C10 and that, before any in-weight route is committed, the program exhausts (1) the literature it already holds, (2) the literature it has not yet reached, and (3) the connections between them. The question that decides the route is narrow: does any published work write a coined, never-seen multi-word value into weights by locate-then-edit and score it on held-out paraphrases, and if not, which mechanism is closest and what is its falsifier here.

**Answer after part 1, the deciding check on nineteen full texts, and the first part-2 passes: the cell is still empty.** Nineteen papers were read at the evaluation section (section numbers in §3); none writes a coined multi-token name into weights and scores held-out paraphrase recall. Two papers come closest from outside the locate-then-edit family: *Fresh in memory* (fine-tunes Llama-3.2-1B on 16,000 entities whose names are replaced by unique three-token random aliases such as `sjdhf` and five-token phrases such as `prickly cyan mouse`, then probes held-out entities and an unseen QA template, §2 and Table 1) and *Can LMs Learn New Entities from Descriptions?* (real post-cutoff entities injected by fine-tuning or MEND, probed by inference questions and perplexity, Table 3). Both are fine-tuning routes, outside the engine-untouched constraint, and both are calibration material for the admission test rather than solutions.

What the arc did find is three mechanism ingredients that the program's own knob sweeps never touched, each with a pre-registrable falsifier, and one admission-test predictor:

## 2. Part 1 — what we already hold (Connected Papers abstracts)

The method's useful output here was the retrieval and research-ideas passes, not the gap structure; the difference calls in both directions were dominated by set-size and vocabulary artifacts and are recorded as leads only.

**Calibration (advisor issue 5).** The planted-positive test asks whether the pipeline surfaces a paper that matters and that no query term named. It did, modestly: the research-ideas pass on the recent uncited graph named *Diagnosing Model Editing via Knowledge Spectrum* as "an underweighted thread" and *Fine-tuning Done Right* as a live baseline, neither of which was in the plan's candidate list; the pass on the older graph re-derived SWEA's role unprompted. It did not surface *Mitigating Heterogeneous Token Overfitting*, which was found by the title scan of the second ring. Reading: the graphs are a weak discovery tool here and a reasonable prioritiser; the arc's value came from the full-text reads they pointed at.

**P1-Q1 (mechanisms for multi-token or novel targets).** OverTone (I-1), Forward Replay (I-2), SWEA (I-3), Latent Knowledge Scalpel (arXiv 2508.03741: replaces entity representations in the latent space the way one edits an input), DOW-KE (anchor-free end-to-end weight optimization, already in the sweep). **P1-Q2 (interference, superposition).** *Knowledge in Superposition* (arXiv 2408.07413: an interference term in the closed-form lifelong solution tied to superposition between facts; activations taken at the subject's last token), *Superimposed Noise Accumulation* / DeltaEdit, *Subspace-Aware Key-Value Mappings* (SUIT, arXiv 2509.24502: keys and values computed only in the subspace of critical features; §6.1 reduces perturbation at the entity's last token), *Related Knowledge Perturbation* (I-5). **P1-Q3 (continuation objective).** OverTone is the only paper found that puts an objective on the later target tokens directly. **P1-Q4.** Structurally the cited set lacks the neuron/subspace vocabulary the uncited set carries (SWEA, subspace, token dimension); the uncited set lacks the program's lifecycle vocabulary (null space, anchor, codebook).

## 5. Part 3 — convergence (first pass)
