## 1. What the paper shows (EVIDENCE-SHOWS, from the paper)

- **Object:** for a relation r, the transformer's map from the subject representation `s` to the object `o` is approximated by an affine map `LRE(s) = β·W_r·s + b_r`, with `W_r` the **mean Jacobian** `∂F/∂s` over n examples of the relation and `b_r` the matching bias (§3.1, eq. 2–4). `s` is read **at the last token of the subject** at a layer ℓ_r, "following insights from Meng et al. (2022) and Geva et al. (2023)" (footnote 5). `β > 1` corrects a magnitude underestimate caused by layer norms (β = 2.25 on GPT-J for best causality, Fig. 6). n = 8 in-context examples (5 for LLaMA-13B); the project page says 5 samples suffice.
- **Two metrics.** *Faithfulness*: does `argmax D(LRE(s))` equal the model's own next-token prediction (first object token only). *Causality*: invert the LRE (low-rank pseudoinverse `W_r†`, rank ρ_r) to find `Δs` such that patching `s + Δs` at the subject's last token makes the model emit a different object `o′` (§3.2, eq. 6–8; Fig. 2). Hyperparameters ℓ_r, ρ_r, β are chosen per relation for best causal influence.
- **Dataset and models.** 47 relations in four groups (factual, commonsense, implicit bias, linguistic); GPT-J primary, GPT-2-XL and LLaMA-13B in Appendix H. The factual set includes `country capital city` (24 examples), `country largest city`, `country language`, `country currency`, `city in country`, `landmark in country` (Table, Appendix A).
- **Headline results.** "Over 60 % faithfulness for almost half of the relations" (§4); faithfulness and causality correlate at R = 0.84 when hyperparameters are chosen for causality, and causality is usually the higher of the two (Fig. 6). LRE beats Logit-Lens, translation (`s + b`), linear regression, and LRE-on-the-raw-embedding baselines (Fig. 4): both the projection and the bias are necessary.
- **Non-linear relations exist.** "No method reaches over 6 % faithfulness on the Company CEO relation, despite GPT-J accurately predicting the CEOs of 69 companies" (§4); the project page generalises this to relations with large ranges (names of people and companies). The model decodes these correctly by a non-linear route.
- **Layer dependence.** Faithfulness is high over a window of layers and then "plummets" (example: layers 7–17 on GPT-J, Fig. 7); the authors hypothesise a "mode switch" from current-word information to next-token information (§4.3).
- **Scope limits they state.** First object token only (Appendix I); results are on base models of 1.5B–13B.

## 2. Bearing on Route A and the C10 line (I-INFER unless marked; nothing here is a result)

**2.1 The keying locus, and why last-prompt-token keying gave ~0 paraphrase transfer.** LRE reads and perturbs `s` at the **subject's last token**: the same locus ROME/MEMIT key their updates on, and the locus where LRE causality shows a perturbation flips the object across the relation's contexts. AnyEdit (`AlphaEdit_ARE`/`memit_ARE`) keys its edit at the **last prompt token** and at answer-internal positions (`compute_z.py:91`), with no subject-token keying and no context templates. Route A's back-test measured exactly the consequence: at the z-layer the edit moves the canonical prompt's hidden state by 100 % of the request and the paraphrases' by 0.8–1.8 % (`results/c10k_route_a/solve_backtest.json`, Amendment 3 §6e). In LRE's frame: the relation is applied to `s` by computation downstream of the subject position; a write keyed at the template's last token sits *after* that computation in a context the paraphrases do not reproduce, so it cannot be read back from them. This is consistent with, and gives a mechanism for, the HALTED result; it does not by itself prove that subject-token keying would rescue A7 (our MEMIT runs already key at the subject token and A7 still fails, CORPUS/36–39).

**2.2 Open-range named values are where linear decoding fails.** The relation that defeats every linear approximation in the paper is `Company CEO` (< 6 % faithfulness), an open-range person-name object. Our fragile class is **project-coined multi-word semantic values** (A7; CORPUS/36), also open-range name-like objects with no prior. If Qwen2.5-3B decodes such relations non-linearly from `s`, a rank-one-per-key linear write at `s` (MEMIT) or at the template token (AnyEdit) can fit the trained prompt without installing a linearly readable relation, which is the fit-then-fail pattern C10 recorded. This is the first external mechanism that predicts the *value-class* dependence we measured (single-token and prior-coherent values fine, coined multi-word values not), rather than a knob-level explanation.

**2.3 A cheap, pre-registerable predictor.** LRE faithfulness is a few forward passes plus Jacobians at the band layers (one SVD-free computation per relation). A falsifiable next step for the program: *LRE faithfulness of the target relation on Qwen2.5-3B at band [4–8] predicts held-out-paraphrase `para_full` after an in-weight edit.* Positive control: fictional-country → single-token capital (A1; expect high faithfulness, matches 94 % generalisation). Test: coined-subject → coined multi-word value (A7; predict low). If the prediction holds, "is this relation linearly decodable from the subject on this model" becomes an admission test for in-weight writes and a routing rule (linear → in-weight; non-linear → index/side-store), which is a Layer-6 lifecycle decision on the e2e map (`docs/SPEC_E2E_GROUND_TRUTH.md`), not a write-engine knob.

**2.4 Layer window.** The paper's "linearly decodable then plummets" layer profile is measurable on our model in minutes and would give a principled band for the write (C10e/C10f found later and wider bands worse, CORPUS/37–38; LRE's mode-switch picture predicts exactly that the useful window is bounded above).

**2.5 What not to over-read.** LRE evaluates the **first object token only**; our multi-token continuation wall (C10, CORPUS/35) is outside its scope. LRE is a description of the unedited model's decoding, not an editing method; its causality edits are activation patches, not weight edits.
