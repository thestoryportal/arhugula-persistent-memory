Role-decomposed editing: what retrieval and video already know that C10 doesn't

The shared failure, stated precisely

Three literatures, one pathology: a single anchor cannot survive reformulation of its context.

C10: write anchored at one token (last prompt), one band, one value. Paraphrase 0/72. Hidden-state transfer 0.8–1.8% at any ridge — the anchor simply isn't reconstructed by the reformulated prompt.
Naïve RAG: one index (dense or sparse). Query reformulation moves the embedding off the anchor; recall collapses. The RAGPart/RAGMask finding is the dual — retrieval anchors on literal tokens, not task structure, which is exactly your keygram rank-1.02 collapse in a different currency.
Autoregressive video: context anchored on the tail alone. Error accumulates over rollout because short-term continuity is the only anchor there is.

The naming in your own graph is almost embarrassing: `edit ↔ single` carries the labels paraphrase, token, subject, multus, band. The single/multus axis is the live variable, and it's categorical.

---

What the two solved cases did

Hybrid RAG (Blended RAG, HyPA-RAG, HetaRAG). They did not tune a single retriever harder. They refused to pick one index: dense + sparse + KG, blended by a query-complexity classifier that routes adaptively. The gain comes from heterogeneous anchors with a routing rule — not from better anchors.

Relax Forcing. Three functionally distinct roles: Sink (global stability), Tail (short-term continuity), History (mid-range motion). The selection criterion is the sharp part — History frames are chosen to maximise alignment with global anchors while suppressing redundancy with recent context. That is a diversity objective over memory slots, training-free.

Both are the same move: role-decompose the anchor, add a selection rule that penalises redundancy.

Carrier-as-pointer: the concrete build

The design already exists in your corpus — it's just filed under the wrong problem

Route (ii)'s published construction: multi-token targets keyed at subject-side anchors with a per-target carrier to keep keys distinct, written by the closed-form solve the program already runs. That carrier is currently justified as key hygiene — a way to stop two multi-token targets colliding in key space. Re-read it as payload substitution and it becomes the fix for P(full | first) = 0.51.

The move:

What you write: a single, high-prior, prior-coherent token — the carrier — at the subject-last-token site. This is the 97.2% / P(full|first)=1.00 regime. You are writing into the arm that already works.
What you don't write: "Amsterdam Ankara". The arbitrary string never enters W_down. It lives in a side-store, keyed by carrier.
What makes it a pointer: the carrier is chosen so the model's own continuation distribution is predictable at the carrier position — not so it means anything.

This reclassifies C10 from can the model store an arbitrary string to can the model reliably emit one token and route on it — and the second question your controls have already answered yes to.

---

Why this respects the falsifier rather than evading it

C10 fired against the value, not the prompt form — the coherent control rebutted prompt-shape overfitting. That verdict survives: arbitrary multi-token values do not generalize under AlphaEdit/band[4-8]/3B. Carrier-as-pointer does not contradict it; it declines the task C10 declared impossible and substitutes one the same recipe passes.

That's the honest framing. It is not a rescue of multi-token payload editing. It is a scope change, and it must be labelled as one in the e2e map: §8.9 write/firing moves from value installation to value routing, with the falsifier left standing as the reason.

The three measurements that decide it

M1 — carrier emission under paraphrase. Write carrier-only, measure held-out-paraphrase first-token. The relevant comparison is not to 36.1% but to 97.2%. If carrier emission lands near the single-token control, the pointer is sound. If it lands near 70.8%, the carrier is behaving like an arbitrary token and the idea is dead — cheap, decisive, one run.

M2 — carrier distinctness at scale. Six carriers, six subjects: do keygram ranks separate, or do you reproduce the rank-1.02 collapse at the carrier layer?
