8.1 Bearing on the keying finding and C10 (READ-NEXT marked ★)
★ PropMEND — Liu et al., arXiv 2506.08920 (Jun 2025); code leo-liuzy/propmend. Hypernetwork editor (MEND lineage) trained so an injected fact answers propagation questions, on Llama-3.2-1B/3B and Qwen2.5-1.5B base. Controlled RippleEdit: in-domain efficacy 64.0 %, OOD-Entity 34.7 %, OOD-Relation 33.3 %, OOD-Both 17.7 %; a 3.4B-parameter hypernetwork with 30k training instances lifts in-domain to 98.5 % "but OOD(Both) remains stuck at 17.8 %"; "editing lower layers is more effective for knowledge propagation". Single-edit only. Q2 (unseen entities are the generalisation cliff on a small Qwen, 34.7 % against our 19–31 % coined-value band); Q1 (lower layers propagate better); Q3 (verbatim vs non-verbatim propagation split). Bearing: an independent, differently-built editor hits the same wall on the same model family, and scaling the editor fixes the trained distribution but not unseen entities — the admission-test / side-store framing of C10 gains support; the knob framing loses it
★ Knowledge Localization and Editability in Small Language Models — Deshpande et al., KnowFM 2026 (ACL Anthology 2026.knowfm-1.13). Causal tracing then ROME at the dominant layer on Gemma-2-2B-IT, Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, Phi-2. Claims the dominant layer for Qwen2.5-3B is 35 of 36 and "attention-dominant (MLP ratio 0.338)"; ROME at that layer succeeds on 3 of 5 editable facts (60 %); success = probability shifts on the trained prompt (e.g. Δpfalse > 0.03 and Δptrue < −0.03); n = 15 test facts per model before filtering; no paraphrase or locality scoring. Q1 (a locus claim that contradicts the program's); Q3 (trained-prompt probability criterion); Q5 ("editability correlates with architectural concentration rather than model size"). Conflict, weighed: the program's own evidence on Qwen2.5-3B base is a full depth map (CORPUS/20, reproduced on this machine to, Δ, ≤ 0.014) and today's K1 cell (band [4–8], subject-keyed, 95.8 % held-out paraphrase transfer, locality intact). A write at layers 4–8 that transfers to paraphrases cannot be reconciled with "the fact lives at layer 35"; the paper's Instruct model, five facts, and probability-shift criterion make it the weaker measurement. Recorded as a conflict, not a correction; a causal-trace replication on the base model is cheap if Route (ii) is chosen and would settle it
Does Knowledge Localization Hold True? — Wei et al., arXiv 2409.00617 (Sep 2024). GPT-2 XL / GPT-J: editing the entity of a triple vs the relation is asymmetric — entity-edit reliability 15.5–31 % across FT/KN/MEND/ROME/MEMIT while relation edits score high; "relational knowledge is also significantly encoded in attention modules". Q1 (what the key denotes decides where the write lands — the entity/relation split is the subject/frame split of today's ablation seen from the other side); Q4. Bearing: supports the keying finding mechanistically; no coined-value data
CoT2Edit — Fu et al., arXiv 2604.05540 (Apr 2026); code FredJDean/CoT2Edit. Non-parametric: SFT + GRPO teach the model to reason over retrieved edited facts. LLaMA3-8B / Falcon3-10B / Qwen3-14B: MQuAKE edit success 99.95 vs AlphaEdit 97.11; CounterFact-uns neighbourhood 93.05 vs LTE 67.18; 85.2 % at 10k edits; paraphrase "↑∼10 %" over EditCoT. Q3; Q4 (no collapse because facts live in retrieval); Q6. Bearing: the best-benchmarked version of Route (iii) — but 8B+ models and a GRPO stage; shrinking it to 3B is unproven
★ AdaTKG — Lee et al. (LG AI Research), arXiv 2605.07121, KDD 2026 workshop; code seunghan96/AdaTKG (Apache-2.0). Temporal-KG reasoning with a per-entity online memory me(τ) = α·me(τ−1) + (1−α)·xe(τ), one shared scalar α, zero-mask so an unseen entity "never pays a cold-start cost" (reduces exactly to the TransFIR backbone). MRR on emerging entities: ICEWS14 0.2011 vs 0.1763 (+14.1 %), ICEWS18 0.1379 vs 0.1114 (+23.8 %). Q2 (emerging entities with no training-time parameters); Q6 (fixed backbone + externally updated per-entity memory). Bearing: the shape Route (iii) needs for coined values — a design pattern to port, not an LLM memory
When Successful Knowledge Graph Edits Displace Correct Answers — Lai et al., arXiv 2609.12116 (Sep 2026); code pa0lai/kge-rank-locality. Editing KG-embedding models (DistMult/ComplEx, FB15k-237): promotion succeeds without displacing correct answers in only 23.0–23.2 % of edits; strict preservation succeeds in 1.3–1.4 %; support-regularised editing 36.3–37.7 %; "mean null-space dimension is only 1.125". Q4 (parameter-support locality is not locality); Q6. Bearing: an embedding KG side store has its own locality problem; if Route (iii) is taken, prefer a symbolic store or audit rank displacement
The Missing Piece in Model Editing (GORA/SORA) — Wang et al., arXiv 2403.07825 (Mar 2024). GPT2-XL: hidden-space ripple to unrelated facts exceeds same-entity ripple ("GORA reduced by 16.51 %"); re-editing the top-5 outlier triplets cuts the damage 54.75 %. Q4; Q6 (a knowledge graph as the audit structure for what an edit disturbed). Bearing: a verify-step pattern for the lifecycle
A Comprehensive Study of Knowledge Editing (KnowEdit / EasyEdit) — Zhang et al., arXiv 2401.01286 v4 (Mar 2024). LLaMA-2-7b-chat: "edit success includes both the text itself and paraphrases"; portability universally low; ROME 96.57 % success / 27.14 % locality on ZsRE; "dramatic performance drops at 1,000 sequential edits". Q3 (the field's success-with-paraphrase vs portability split); Q4; Q6 (external-memory vs weight-modifying taxonomy). Bearing: a scoring cross-check; no coined values
Abstain / verify primitives (Q6 lifecycle): SEAT (Shen et al., 2506.14387 v3; Llama3-8B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) — sparse updates plus an entity-perturbed KL regulariser ("replacing the subject entity, keeping the remaining context unchanged"), abstention 0.95–0.98 after injection; the subject-swap control is directly adoptable in the keying ablation (Q1). 9.1 Items (★ = direct C10 analogue or a new mechanism class)
★ GenRecEdit — Shen et al., SIGIR 2026, arXiv 2603.14259 v2; code Starrylay/GenRecEdit (POS2LAYER=(0 1 2 3)). The closest published analogue to C10: cold-start items in a TIGER-style generative recommender (T5, 6 decoder layers) are unseen 4-token semantic IDs, injected with the MEMIT-form closed-form update ΔW = R K₁ᵀ(λC₀ + K₁K₁ᵀ)⁻¹ into an otherwise frozen model. Mechanism: one edit per target position, each at its own layer (chosen by a linear probe that separates cold-start keys; "intermediate-to-early layers tend to yield higher probing accuracy"), with one-one triggering so that generating position p activates only layer lp's edit. Ablation: without one-one triggering, IID Ratio@10 falls 0.7359 → 0.0030 — concurrent per-position edits annihilate each other. Cold-start NDCG@10 0.0028 → 0.0064 at a 6.5 % warm-item cost; λ ∈ {3000, 3000, 1000} per dataset. Keys are taken at the last token of "the item history together with the prefix of the cold-start item", i.e. a prefix/frame key, and paraphrase transfer is never tested. Q2, Q7 (the per-position annihilation is the batched-near-identical-key interference the keying ablation measured, seen from the target side), Q1, Q4, Q5. Local feasibility: plausible — a scheduling wrapper (position → layer, gated application) around the vendored solve, engine untouched. Bearing: a concrete template for Route (ii) — separate the 2nd..Nth token writes by layer and gate them at inference — with the subject-keyed variant still ours to build and test on paraphrases
★ OmniPersona — Zhong et al., arXiv 2601.06965 (Jan 2026) and ★ UniCTokens — An et al., NeurIPS 2025, arXiv 2505.14671; code arctanxarc/unictokens. Multimodal personalization: a new concept is stored as new tokens with learned embeddings (<sks> plus 32 learnable tokens per concept in OmniPersona), not as a weight edit; OmniPersona's ablations: recognition 0.852 → 0.804 without disentangled tokens, personalised-generation score 0.613 → 0.312 (−49.1 %) without the external text "knowledge replay". Vision-language systems on H20 GPUs, not runnable here; the mechanism class is portable. Q2, Q6. Bearing: a fourth option for C10 — mint one token per coined value so the value becomes single-token for MEMIT (the solved case, 97–99 %) — with the literature's own evidence that an external text store then carries most of the attribute recall
Persuasion Tokens (P-Tokens) — Youssef et al., arXiv 2601.16781 v2; code paulyoussef/p-tokens (--modelname Qwen/Qwen2.5-7B). Two learned delimiter embeddings make the model trust an in-context edit statement without demonstrations; GPT-J-6B, Qwen2.5-7B/14B, Llama3-8B; CounterFact efficacy 95.82 vs IKE 93.97, paraphrase ≈ 98–99 %, 16× shorter prompts, 5× faster than IKE; neighbourhood drops 10–15 pp with 100-edit distractors; 15.5 h training on 7B-class GPU. The object sits verbatim in context, so any coined multi-token value is read at ~98 %. Local feasibility: yes — a config change to Qwen2.5-3B base, engine untouched. Q2, Q3, Q4, Q6. Bearing: the ready-made Route (iii) baseline that quantifies what a side store buys over weights for the C10 value class
OnceEdit — Ma et al., arXiv 2506.00817 (Jun 2025). A small plug-in model (Tiny-Llama) is fully fine-tuned with the edits and fused at every decoding step with the frozen target (α predicted per instance; vocabulary transfer matrices); Llama2-7B/ZsRE reliability 0.99 / generality 0.92 / locality 0.99 (teacher-forced) vs WISE 0.84 / 0.78 / 0.99; one edit serves Llama2-7B, Mistral-7B, GPT-J-6B, Qwen2.5-7B, Llama3-70B; sequential editing not explored. Multi-token values are emitted token by token by the plug-in, so the target needs no per-token key at all. Local feasibility: plausible (Tiny-Llama fine-tune + logit fusion with Qwen2.5-3B; needs a Qwen↔Llama vocabulary map). Q6, Q3 (teacher-forced vs generation gap noted), Q4, Q5. Bearing: Route (iii) where the store is a small editable model
MELO — Yu et al., AAAI 2024, arXiv 2312.11795; code ECNU-ICALK/MELO (CUDA PEFT fork). Non-overlapping LoRA blocks per edit batch, routed by a vector database of last-hidden-state keys; T5-Small zsRE edit success 0.98 / generality 0.97 / locality 0.72; 2,464 edits/min; rank and radius hand-set per model (rank 4 BERT, 2 T5/GPT-2; radius 1.0–75.0). The key is a gate over a learned adapter, not the write key, so its last-token locus does not contradict the keying finding. Q1, Q4, Q5, Q6, Q7 (batch isolation by construction). Bearing: Route (iii) as a key-gated adapter store; porting cost moderate
DAFNet — Zhang et al., arXiv 2405.20588 (May 2024). Meta-learned sequential editor that decomposes each edit's gradient into one rank-1 term per target token, fused by token-level attention into one update of the last three FFN layers; LLaMA-2 100 sequential edits reliability 89.5 / generality 76.5 / locality 90.2 vs MALMEN 50.6 / 40.7 / 59.3; releases DAFSet (103k samples with a long-tail / low-frequency split). Not runnable here (hypernetwork training on 6–7B, CUDA). Q2, Q3, Q4, Q5, Q6. Bearing: the clearest prior statement that every target token should contribute its own term — Route (ii)'s intuition in gradient form; DAFSet's long-tail split is a Route (i) admission-signal source
Learning to Edit (LTE) — Jiang, PhD thesis, arXiv 2506.09329 (Jun 2025). Alignment-phase fine-tune + retrieval of edit descriptors at inference on LLaMA2-Chat-7B and Qwen-Chat-7B; numbers not extractable from the thesis HTML (take them from the ACL 2024 LTE paper). Q6, Q4. Bearing: Route (iii) with a meta-trained reader
Automatically Finding and Validating Unexpected Side-Effects — Pope et al., arXiv 2605.05090 (May 2026). Contrastive free-generation audit: an LLM proposes hypotheses about how a pre- and post-intervention model differ, a blinded judge scores held-out responses, Mann–Whitney U with Benjamini–Hochberg q = 0.05; ROME on Llama3-8B yields 41 ± 12 validated side-effect hypotheses (AUC 0.626); ≈ $0.09 per hypothesis. Q3, Q4. Bearing: a locality instrument that would have surfaced the keying ablation's frame bleed as a validated hypothesis rather than a hand-written bystander probe; candidate implementation of the new same-frame locality requirement
MCircKE — Zhao et al., arXiv 2604.05876 v3. Circuit discovery (EAP-IG) then LoRA inside the circuit; GPT-2 L/XL, GPT-J, Qwen2.5-7B; ROME's single→multi-hop collapse 61.5 → 12.5 %; "for multi-token bridge entities, we use the first token of the entity". Q1, Q3, Q4, Q5. Bearing: the first-token shortcut is the one the program is trying to get past; otherwise none
QRNCA — Chen et al., arXiv 2406.10868 v4. Query-relevant FFN neurons for long-form text, scored by collapsing the answer to a multiple-choice letter; domain neurons in Llama-2-7B layers 15–18. Q1, Q3. Bearing: a cautionary metric that hides the multi-token problem; none for C10

9.2 C10 candidate-solution map (I-INFER; what this pass adds to the forward decision)
The keying ablation established that where a write is keyed decides what is written. This pass supplies four ways to get a 4–6-token coined value into or next to the weights, each with a cheap pre-registrable falsifier on the M3 Pro:
option, mechanism (source), runs locally, engine untouched?, falsifier to pre-register, what a PASS would mean
(ii-a) position-separated subject-keyed write, GenRecEdit's one-edit-per-position-per-layer with one-one triggering, re-keyed at the subject's last token (DAFNet's per-token terms as the gradient analogue), plausible — a scheduler around the vendored solve; multi-day, A7 coined-coined with per-position layers L4…L8, A1/A2 controls, held-out paraphrase full-sequence; GenRecEdit predicts collapse without gating, in-weight multi-token values at ≥ 85 % held-out paraphrase — C10 closed in-weight
(iv) mint a token per coined value, OmniPersona / UniCTokens: new token + learned embedding (init from constituent-token mean; lmhead row likewise), then the ordinary subject-keyed MEMIT write of a now single-token value, yes — tokenizer/embedding resize is harness-side; the GGUF path must carry the added tokens, A7 values minted as one token each vs the same values as 4–6 tokens; prediction from A1 (97 %): ≥ 90 % paraphrase if the wall is the span, not the novelty, C10 reduces to the solved single-token case plus a tokenizer-management requirement; spec question for the operator: a minted token is a syntax-level artefact the Git layer would have to own
(iii) side store, quantified, P-Tokens (config change to Qwen2.5-3B), OnceEdit (plug-in), MELO (gated LoRA), LTE (retrieved descriptors), P-Tokens yes; OnceEdit/MELO plausible, A7 values via P-Tokens on Qwen2.5-3B base: predicted ≈ 98 % paraphrase vs 19–31 % in-weight; locality under 100 distractors, the number the accept-bounded decision needs: what the side store buys and costs
locality instrument, Pope et al. contrastive audit; same-frame prompts (keying ablation), yes, run on any of the above, catches frame bleed and distractor erosion that described-as probes miss
Recommendation (I-INFER): (iv) is the cheapest decisive test — it asks whether the C10 wall is the multi-token span or the novelty of the value, a question the knob sweeps never separated — and it should be pre-registered before the heavier (ii-a). P-Tokens should run alongside as the Route (iii) yardstick. Whether a minted token is admissible under the spec's syntax/semantics split (§7.1) is the operator's call and should be decided before (iv) runs

10.1 Items (★ = construction the program needs, or a novel-entity data source)
★ FOVEATED: Improving Atomic-Fact Recall via Focused Views in Unstructured Knowledge Editing — Wu, Zhang et al., arXiv 2610.02772 (Oct 2026; PDF). Diagnoses "context-induced difficulty underestimation": under teacher forcing, pre-edit NLL of new facts falls toward later passage positions (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, UnFine), so editors under-learn them and they are unrecoverable without the passage. Fix: during editing only, randomly shift the key-side RoPE positions of each preceding context block (Theorem 3.1: mean-margin reduction + view-consistency KL). For locate-then-edit editors (µKE, UnKE) a two-stage write: Stage 1 optimises one residual per sentence at layer-6 MLP outputs at "query anchors" — prompt tokens whose log-prob the passage lifts (gp > 1.0, confidence > 0.3), "excluding the final prompt position", "a query-side proxy for the entity or concept being edited" — plus a temporary sentence-specific carrier token, then writes all sentence targets jointly into layers 4–6 with AlphaEdit's closed-form null-space solver (λ₂ = 10; 20k Wikipedia null-space samples); Stage 2 runs the base editor with an anchor-preservation MSE. Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct; atomic ROUGE-L "+4.4 points on average in all 30 scenarios", rephrased-query recall "+8.4 in 19 of 20", LTE editors "+12.9 % atomic ROUGE-L"; ablation: sentence-only writes collapse Orig/Reph Q 99.9/85.5 → 40.1/38.5; general capability within 4.3 F1 points. Scores Orig Q, Reph Q and Atom Q (separately asked atomic questions, passage absent, greedy decoding) — the decomposition metric the program lacks; novel entity: counterfactual passages, no coined names. Q1 (anchors are subject-side by construction; final position excluded), Q2 (per-sentence residual + carrier), Q3, Q4, Q7 (shared anchors kept distinct by the per-sentence carrier). Local feasibility: plausible — the RoPE perturbation is a forward-pass change; Stage 1 is AlphaEdit-shaped (anchor keys, preservation keys, ridge, projector) and could be driven as a wrapper around the vendored solve; Stage 2's whole-block editors are the heavy part. Bearing: the published construction for Route (ii) — multi-token targets keyed at subject-side anchors with a per-target carrier to keep keys distinct, written by the closed-form solve the program already runs; its carrier token is a cousin of option (iv); its pre-edit NLL-by-position curve is a cheap admission signal for Route (i). Untested below 7B and on coined names: C10 stays open, but the design space is no longer empty
★ AKEW: Assessing Knowledge Editing in the Wild — Wu et al., EMNLP 2024, arXiv 2402.18909 v2; data bobxwu/AKEW. Same edit in three formats (structured triplet, unstructured passage, extracted triplets) over CounterFact (975), MQuAKE-CF (436) and WikiUpdate: 1,067 real Wikidata updates after 2021-04-01, i.e. objects the base models have not seen. FT falls 97.33 % → 0.07 % structured→unstructured on GPT2-XL; ROME on WikiUpdate/GPT-J 99.63 % structured vs 2.33 % on extracted triplets; "the locate-then-edit methods, ROME and MEMIT, are unable to handle unstructured facts, because they require triplets". Scores editing accuracy only. Q2, Q3, Q4, Q6. Bearing: the only item in this pass with genuinely novel multi-token entities — a ready calibration set for the Route (i) admission test (do real post-2021 names reproduce the 19–31 % cliff, and does an LRE/faithfulness predictor separate the storable ones?); confirms from outside that locate-then-edit is triplet-bound
CRME — He et al., SSRN 7519289 (2026 preprint; PDF). Frozen-backbone representation memory keyed at the final prompt position, edits clustered by key cosine (τ = 0.9, ≤ 8 per cluster), rank-4 shared memories at layer 15/14 (Llama-3.1-8B / Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct), router activates on cosine ≥ 0.9. Table 1 (UnKEBench BERTScore, original queries): MEMIT 0.708 at 10 edits → 0.029 at 100; AnyEdit 0.452 → 0.012; UnKE 0.649 → 0.380; CRME 1.000. At 1,000 edits paraphrase BERT/ROUGE-L 0.939/0.815 (Llama) and 0.888/0.694 (Qwen) vs MEMIT 0.019/0.028; MMLU: all weight editors fall to ≈ 0.25. No code; data on request. Q1, Q3, Q4, Q6, Q7. Bearing: outside corroboration of the keying finding at scale — the long-form lineage keys at the final token and recovers paraphrases only by retrieval-style key matching, and MEMIT's collapse between 10 and 100 long-form edits is the near-identical-final-token-key interference we measured at N = 24
UnKE — Deng et al., ICLR 2025, arXiv 2405.15349 v3; code TrustedLLM/UnKE. Replaces MEMIT's subject-term key with "cause-driven optimization to directly edit the last token of the input" and the MLP-local store with a whole-block (layer 7) update; LLaMA2-7B-Chat, Qwen1.5-7B-Chat. UnKEBench original/paraphrase BLEU 98.63 / 76.85, BERTScore 99.61 / 93.09 vs MEMIT 35.79 / 33.19; FactScore (sub-question recall) 42.49 vs MEMIT 26.39; batch stable to 2⁶; MMLU −0.1 to −0.4. Q1 (the field's own number for what last-token keying costs on paraphrase), Q3 (three-way split incl. decomposition), Q4, Q5, Q7. Bearing: the canonical final-token long-form editor; its block-level (attention-inclusive) write carries context that our MLP-only last-token write does not — a yardstick, not a fix
★ µKE — Su et al., COLM 2025, arXiv 2504.01196 v2; code PurCL/muke (ships Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct configs). AnyEdit-style chunked editing (20–30-token windows) with a Matryoshka objective over nested downstream windows; memory shift hooked at the last input token. Qwen2.5-7B UnKEBench original BLEU/ROUGE-L/BERTScore 96.34/97.02/99.02 vs MEMIT 45.18/38.21/78.10; AKEW-CounterFact paraphrase BLEU 41.22 (Llama3) — the "lands but reads only from the trained sentence" signature. Q1, Q2, Q3, Q4, Q5, Q7. ★ only as the released Qwen2.5 long-form editor: the obvious port for a last-token-keyed control run; negative example for Route (ii)
COIN — Zhou et al., arXiv 2602.19043 (Feb 2026). Names "Context Reliance" in unstructured editing: gradient editing binds knowledge "to aggregated contextual representations rather than semantically relevant tokens alone" (Theorem 3.2); late-position facts lose up to 56 % ROUGE vs early ones; adds context-alignment and consistency losses at MLP layer 21 on Llama3-8B / Qwen2.5-7B. Scores sub-question ROUGE-L/BERTScore; no coined names. Q1 (the gradient-side statement of the keying finding), Q3, Q4. Bearing: Route (i) — context-bound facts are predictably unreadable out of context; the position diagnostic is cheap to replicate against our engine
HoReN — Fang et al., arXiv 2605.08143 (May 2026); code ha11ucin8/HoReN. Codebook of normalised keys with one damped Hopfield step wrapped around a frozen MLP layer; value added at the last prompt token; 1.5B–32B models incl. Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct; ZsRE N = 1000 Rel/Gen/Loc 0.99/0.93/0.99; at 50k edits ≥ 0.93 while "AlphaEdit cliffs from 0.82 at 2K to 0.08 at 10K"; unnormalised keys collapse locality to ≤ 0.08.
